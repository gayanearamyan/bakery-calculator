# Bake Day Calculator 🥖

Your bakery's planning tool. Open the calculator here: **https://gayanearamyan.github.io/bakery-calculator/**

Use it before every bake day to know exactly what to buy, what it costs, and what you'll earn.

## How to use it

**1. Enter your order.**
At the top, set how many of each item you're baking this week — sourdough loaves, butter tarts, cookies, fruit tarts. Use the +/− buttons or type the number.

**2. Read your numbers.**
The cards below update instantly:
- **Revenue** — what you'll collect from customers
- **Ingredients used** — what those items truly cost you to make
- **Net profit** — what stays in your pocket, with your margin %
- **Cash to shop** — how much money to bring to the store

**3. Take the shopping ticket to the store.**
It lists every ingredient: how much you need, how many packages to buy, and the cost. Press **Print bake sheet** to print just the ticket and summary for the fridge.

**4. Check profit by item.**
The breakdown table shows which items earn you the most — useful when deciding what to promote or what to price differently.

## Make it yours

- **Recipes** — the amounts per item are starting estimates. After you bake a real batch, weigh your ingredients and update the numbers so your costs become exact. You can add or remove ingredient lines, rename recipes, and change selling prices.
- **Pantry prices** — update these whenever store prices change (this is what keeps your profit numbers honest). Add new ingredients here first, then use them in a recipe.
- **Packaging & other costs** — set your per-item packaging cost (bags, stickers, labels) and add one-off costs like delivery or gas per order.
- Your changes **save automatically** in your browser — next time you open the page, everything is as you left it. **Reset to defaults** brings back the starting numbers if you ever want a clean slate.
- Click the bakery name at the top to rename it. ✨

## Two numbers that look similar but aren't

- **Cost used** = the ingredients this order actually consumes. This is what's subtracted from revenue to get your profit.
- **Cost to buy** = whole packages at the store (you can't buy 325 g of flour). The leftovers aren't wasted — they carry over to your next bake, so over time your real spending gets closer to "cost used."
